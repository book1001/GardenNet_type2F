Description

In this introductory course for sophomore graphic design majors, students will study the fundamentals of typography, from its history and anatomy to organizational structures, legibility and hierarchy. Legibility and hierarchy will be examined with increasing complexity from the letterform, to the word, sentence, paragraph and page. Students will learn characteristics of major type families, how to create and use a typographic grid and to work with type in a dynamic environment.



Learning Outcomes

  • Study typographic terminology
  • Explore type as an independent design element
  • Cultivate typographic sensibilities
  • Develop grid systems as organizational tools

🄋 Type Research

Typography is a fundamental element of graphic design, and understanding a wide range of typefaces is key to expanding a graphic design practice. Type Research is an ongoing research project presented once during the semester on a selected sign-up date. One serif and one sans serif typeface will be selected from Fonts In Use, an independent typography archive. The history, characteristics, and use of each typeface will be researched and presented to the class.





➊ Letterform Study

As the first project, Letterform Study introduces the history and classification of typefaces while examining the visual forms and characteristics of letters. These fundamentals of typographic practice will be applied through typesetting exercises, with particular attention to kerning, spacing, and relationships between letterforms. For the final outcome, two typefaces significant to the history of typography will be selected and presented through type specimen posters that demonstrate their letterforms and typesetting.





➋ Typographic Voice

The second project explores how tone and rhythm in writing can be interpreted through typography. Raymond Queneau’s Exercises in Style retells an ordinary incident on a bus in 99 different styles of writing. Twenty-four of these styles will be selected and given a distinct typographic interpretation that responds to the voice, tone, and rhythm of each text. The final outcome will be a letter-size, spiral-bound book presenting all 24 styles, along with a cover, table of contents, and colophon.





❸ Modular Typeface

For the final project of Typography 1, a modular typeface will be designed from a self-developed system. Rather than drawing each letterform independently from scratch, a modular typeface is constructed from a consistent set of forms based on a defined grid or structure, often allowing for more experimental approaches to letterform design. The final outcome will be an eight-page type specimen zine that presents the complete alphabet and demonstrates the typeface in use.
