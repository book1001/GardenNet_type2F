Description

In this introductory course for sophomore graphic design majors, students will study the fundamentals of typography, from its history and anatomy to organizational structures, legibility and hierarchy. Legibility and hierarchy will be examined with increasing complexity from the letterform, to the word, sentence, paragraph and page. Students will learn characteristics of major type families, how to create and use a typographic grid and to work with type in a dynamic environment.



Learning Outcomes

  • Study typographic terminology
  • Explore type as an independent design element
  • Cultivate typographic sensibilities
  • Develop grid systems as organizational tools

Your health and well-being are important for your journey as a professional graphic designer. It is important to work hard and balance that with rest, self-care, and attention to your mental and physical health. 🄋 Type Research

Knowing what is feasible in programming greatly expands the range of ideas you can explore in your projects. Interactive Design is a collaborative space where every class member supports one another. To grow our coding knowledge, we will operate a Collaborative Codebox Are.na channel. Each member will sign up for a date and share five code-related resources they find interesting or new. These resources will be posted on the Are.na channel so that everyone can use them as assets for future projects. The five resources can be anything you think will be helpful to yourself and the class. For example, they could be an online CSS generator, a new JavaScript library, a sample code snippet you discovered on CodePen, or even an AI code generation tool.





➊ Modular Typeface

For your first project, you will select one list from the options provided below and create a publication based on it. The key to this project is how you interpret the list and how you translate that interpretation into design. The form of your publication is completely open. Depending on your interpretation, your publication can be highly experimental, classical, or take any other approach that you find appropriate for the content.





➋ Type Specimen

Based on the workshops from Project 1, you will create a single-page web zine (or multiple pages if you are comfortable with programming). The choice of text and its length are entirely up to you—it can be something you’ve written before or a new piece you would like to explore. It is important to apply interactions that suit your text in order to strengthen the narrative and create a more engaging experience for the reader. You are encouraged to use interactions that were not included in Project 1, or to combine multiple interactions together.





❸ Inventory Catalog

For your final project, you will focus on developing the overall system and structure of a publication. Your publication should include at least five different types of page layouts. The choice of text is open, but you should select a text that has enough volume and complexity to allow you to explore a variety of page layouts and typographic structures. Texts such as encyclopedia entries, instructions, or exhibition catalog texts can be good examples, as they often contain different types and levels of information that can be organized in various ways.
